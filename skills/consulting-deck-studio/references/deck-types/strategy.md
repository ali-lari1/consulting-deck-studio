# Strategy deck types

Strategy decks answer a question the board cares about with an argued recommendation. They lean on external facts
(markets, competitors, economics), so run web research when the agent has it. Cite every external number with its
source and date in `facts.md`. Never present a hypothesis as a finding: label work in progress "Hypothesis" or
"Preliminary".

---

## 1. Strategic diagnostic / situation assessment
**Purpose:** a shared, fact-based view of where the organisation stands and why. **Length:** 12-20 slides.

**Default sequence:**
1. `title`
2. `exec-summary`
3. `key-numbers` (the four or five facts that frame everything)
4. `issue-tree` (the question broken down, with hypothesis status on the leaves)
5. One section per branch, each with 2-4 exhibit slides (`bar`, `line`, `benchmark`, `marimekko`) and a section
   `exec-summary` or divider carrying the message
6. `quotes` (what leaders and customers said, attributed by role)
7. `columns` (implications)
8. `process-flow` (next steps)

**Mandatory:** the question in one sentence, the scope and what's out of it, the sources for every external figure,
and the hypothesis status visible.

**Ask about:** the question as the CEO would put it, the leading hypotheses, what has been tried, and the peers.

## 2. Growth strategy and options
**Default sequence:**
1. `exec-summary` (the recommendation first)
2. `bar` or `line`: the growth gap (ambition against momentum)
3. `marimekko` or `matrix-2x2`: where to play
4. `options-matrix`: options against weighted criteria
5. `waterfall`: a bridge from today's revenue to the target, by initiative
6. `roadmap`
7. `columns`: risks and what we'd need to believe
8. `decisions`

**Mandatory:** the baseline ("momentum") case, the size of the gap, the economics of each option (investment, payback),
and the "what you'd need to believe" for the preferred option.

**Pitfalls:** options that are really one option plus two straw men, and a bridge that doesn't reconcile (it fails
the build).

## 3. Market entry / market attractiveness
**Default sequence:**
1. `exec-summary`
2. `key-numbers`: market size and growth, with sources
3. `marimekko`: market by segment and competitor share
4. `scatter` or `matrix-2x2`: attractiveness against ability to win, by segment or country
5. `benchmark` (competitor economics)
6. `options-matrix`: entry modes (build, buy, partner)
7. `timeline` (phasing)
8. `decisions`

**Mandatory:** the market definition (it drives the size), sources and years for every market number, and the
right-to-win argument.

## 4. Commercial due diligence summary
**Default sequence:**
1. `exec-summary`: answers to the deal questions, with a confidence level
2. `key-numbers`
3. `line` (market growth)
4. `bar` (the target's share and its trend)
5. `quotes` (customer interviews)
6. `waterfall`: the management plan against our view of revenue
7. `columns`: risks and upsides
8. `table`: red flags

**Mandatory:** the management case against the diligence case, the interview count and mix, and the data cut-off date.

**Pitfalls:** echoing the management case unchallenged, and quoting customers without their permission or role.

## 5. Portfolio strategy
**Default sequence:**
1. `scatter` (businesses on growth against returns, with bubbles for revenue)
2. `matrix-2x2` (attractiveness against position)
3. `table` (role of each business: grow, fix, harvest, exit)
4. `waterfall` (portfolio value bridge)
5. `roadmap`
6. `decisions`

## 6. Economic development / public strategy
**Default sequence:**
1. `key-numbers`
2. `benchmark` (the region against comparable regions)
3. `line` (trends)
4. `issue-tree` or `columns`: priorities
5. `options-matrix` (interventions)
6. `roadmap`
7. `org-chart` (delivery governance)
8. `table` (KPIs and targets)

**Mandatory:** comparators chosen on stated criteria, official statistics with their dates, and the targets' baseline
year.

## 7. Board strategy update / offsite deck
**Default sequence:**
1. `exec-summary`
2. `key-numbers` (progress against the strategy KPIs)
3. `table` (initiatives with RAG)
4. Two or three "big questions", each as a `columns` or `options-matrix` slide for discussion
5. `decisions`

**Pitfalls:** turning it into a status report, and no question that genuinely needs the board.

## 8. Scenario planning
**Default sequence:**
1. `matrix-2x2`: two critical uncertainties give four scenarios, each named
2. `columns`: a narrative per scenario
3. `table`: the implications of each scenario for the business
4. `tornado` or `table`: no-regret moves against bets
5. `table`: signposts to monitor

**Mandatory:** uncertainties that really are independent and high-impact, and signposts with thresholds.
